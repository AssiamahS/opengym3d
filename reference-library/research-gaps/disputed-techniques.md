# Conflicts and distinctions requiring review

- **Dumbbell squat hold:** [StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/dumbbell-squat/) discusses side-held weights, while the inspected GIF shows a shoulder hold. These are separate catalog variations. The GIF is not side-hold evidence.
- **Goblet head versus shaft:** [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/362/goblet-squat/) supplies a head-support image. [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/goblet-squat) describes cupping the top. A [Muscle & Strength staff comment](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/exercises/dumbbell-goblet-squat) describes a shaft hold, but this is weaker than a reviewed demonstration. Preserve `vertical-dumbbell-shaft` as separate, low-confidence evidence. Do not use head-support photos to approve the current MPFB shaft fixture.
- **Kettlebell horns versus handle:** the inspected [goblet images](https://www.strengthlog.com/goblet-squat/) show horn contacts. One/two-hand swings, rack and overhead holds need different contact and wrist analysis.
- **Orientation versus closure:** pronated, supinated and neutral describe orientation; they do not establish a closed thumb, hook, false grip, finger overlap or pressure. Current labels containing `closed` are provisional and explicitly say closure is unresolved.
- **Machine universality:** [Precor RSL0602](https://www.precor.com/en-US/products/RSL0602) product text does not justify assuming every leg press has the same moving seat/footplate arrangement. Model-specific motion needs direct verification.
- **Romanian deadlift endpoint:** [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/dumbbell-romanian-deadlift) cues stopping short of full hip extension, whereas [StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/dumbbell-romanian-deadlift/) returns to standing. Retain the source-specific interpretation for review; do not invent an intermediate numerical angle.

These are identified distinctions, not an exhaustive survey of disagreements. No photograph establishes one universally correct stance, depth or exact joint angle.
