## Findings and claim boundaries

- **Dumbbell head support:** [NASM setup](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/goblet-squat) supports vertical, chest-level, two-hand top cupping. [Muscle & Strength formal instruction](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/exercises/dumbbell-goblet-squat) locates hands beneath the edges and explicitly allows a thumbless alternative. Underside head support is a source-guided interpretation; exact fingers, thumb web, wrist angle and shaft contact remain unknown. Do not turn the optional thumbless variant into a mandatory closed grip.
- **Vertical dumbbell shaft:** The same M&S page's Roger reply dated 2024-01-11 reports holding the vertical handle in two hands. This remains a staff anecdote, separate from formal head-support instructions. Targeted ACE/NSCA and broader searches yielded no stronger formal shaft-hold source in this bounded batch. This is an evidence gap, not proof the variant is invalid. Stacking, overlap, thumb and wrist geometry remain unknown.
- **Kettlebell horns:** [StrongFirst / Pavel Macek](https://www.strongfirst.com/kettlebell-simple-sinister-tips-for-heavy-goblet-squats/) distinguishes the handle/belly junction from the top handle and widest corners. Its bottom-down heavy variant supports the palm heels upward at that junction. Bottom-up horns and belly-cupping are separate alternatives. The text does not resolve individual digit paths or wrist angles. Coordinator CUA inspection of its 450×236 collage found open hands near the junction, then curled fingers along handle sides, then a bottom squat. Resolution and occlusion prevent exact contact mapping.
- **Dumbbell shoulder rack:** [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/dumbbell-front-squat) specifies inward palms at shoulder height and high elbows. [CrossFit](https://www.crossfit.com/essentials/the-dumbbell-front-squat) describes primarily shoulder-supported loading and forward elbows. These related cues do not prove identical load sharing or head/shoulder contact patches. Thumb, fingers and wrist angle remain unknown.
